## Spot vs on-demand (7B params, S15 failure rate)

| 2 | 168 | 2.0 | 1.21 | 0.9938 | spot |
| 8 | 168 | 8.01 | 4.88 | 0.9827 | spot |

Spot looks cheaper per useful hour in these rows, but only if spot capacity for whole gpu-node-8 nodes can actually be obtained, and every reclaim adds a restart that a person may have to watch. Decision (ADR 0004): **main run on on-demand or a reserved capacity booking**; spot only for preemptible team jobs (ladder, evals), which already save on SIGTERM.

## Hot spare node

In simple words: a spare costs one node-hour every hour. It pays for itself only if it saves more than that by cutting each failure's restart from 'launch a new instance' to 'use the spare now'.

| nodes | spare_cost_h | saved_per_h | pays_for_itself | break_even_fails_per_day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1.0 | 0.0 | False | 48.0 |
| 4 | 1.0 | 0.0 | False | 24.0 |
| 8 | 1.0 | 0.01 | False | 12.0 |
| 16 | 1.0 | 0.05 | False | 6.0 |

At this failure rate the spare's 15 minutes saved per failure is worth less than its own node-hour at every size here. Its other value is that the main run never waits for capacity that may not exist. Decision (ADR 0004): keep one hot spare, but let it run preemptible team jobs, so its node-hours are not idle.
